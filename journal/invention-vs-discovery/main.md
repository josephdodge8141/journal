# Do humans create novel ideas through invention, or do we simply discover hidden truths?

## Background
AI recently solved the Navier Stokes problem which sparked a debate around knowledge and it's role in innovation. Terrence Tao published a [light rant](https://mathstodon.xyz/@tao/117244102901892965) followed by an [open letter](https://mathandai.org/) talking about the role of AI in math. The post explored the necessity of "childlike wonder" in "basic, curiousity-driven sciences." Specifically, it suggests that there is concrete value in asking questions where the answer has no obvious benefit or application. The "unhurried, convoluted, and serendipitous" journey towards some arbitrary goal, yields fruitful outcomes, much more plentiful than the goal itself. Systemically, the field of mathematics publicly attached itself to a series of difficult problems to appear directional and goal oriented to appease the capitalist environment. OpenAI solving a Millenium prize problem is impressive, but according to my interpretation of Tao, we are missing out on insight necessary to ask the next invaluably "useless" question.
